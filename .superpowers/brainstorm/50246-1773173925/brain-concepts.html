Two Big Ideas — How Should They Live in the App?

Let's nail down the concept before we get into specifics. Click the direction that resonates most for each idea.

1. Visual Brain Network Component

How should users interact with the neuroscience visually?

A

Post-Check-In "Here's What's Happening"

After a check-in, show a simple brain visualization highlighting the active structures and networks for their specific activation pattern. "Your amygdala is driving right now, and your prefrontal cortex is quieter than usual — that's why decisions feel hard." Educational but contextual — they see it when it's relevant to their state.

B

Tool Explainer — "Why This Works"

When the app recommends a tool, show a mini brain viz explaining the mechanism. "Paced breathing activates your vagus nerve, which tells your amygdala to stand down." Builds trust in the recommendation. Shows up inline on tool pages.

C

Standalone "My Brain" Explorer

A dedicated section where users can explore the full brain network — tap structures, see what they do, how they connect to emotions they've felt. Builds over time with their check-in history. More like a "learn about yourself" tool than an in-the-moment feature.

D

All of the Above — Layered

Start with the post-check-in context (A) as the core experience, add tool explainers (B) on tool pages, and eventually build out the full explorer (C) as a power-user feature. Each layer adds depth without requiring it.

2. Neuroscience Insights / Research Section

What kind of "deeper knowledge" experience are you imagining?

A

Personal Neuroscience Insights

Based on their check-in history: "You've been in rumination loops 4 times this month — that's your default mode network getting stuck. Here's what's happening and what helps break the cycle." Personalized, pattern-based, built from their own data.

B

Browseable Knowledge Base

An interactive encyclopedia of brain networks, neurochemical systems, and how they relate to emotions and tools. Users can explore: "What does the salience network do?" → "It switches your brain between internal focus and external focus" → linked emotions, tools, structures. Graph-powered exploration.

C

Research Hub — "The Science Behind This"

For each activation pattern, emotion, or tool, show the actual research: evidence grades, key findings, citations. "Opposite action has Strong evidence (Linehan 2015, replicated across 12 RCTs)". For users who want to understand the rigor behind the recommendations.

D

Combo: Personal Insights + Explorable Knowledge

Lead with personalized insights from their data (A), with each insight linking deeper into the knowledge base (B) and research (C). Their experience is the entry point; the science is the depth.
